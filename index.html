How I Found She Vibes — and Why It Changed Everything

A participant's story. Written by Bharath S, She Vibes Cohort 0.

I was scrolling LinkedIn one afternoon, somewhere between overwhelmed and numb. AI announcements every hour. Courses everywhere. Another thread about agents, another about MCPs, another about the model that just dethroned the last model. I felt like I was standing at the edge of an ocean, being told to drink it all.

"AI content is an ocean right now. Brilliant, noisy, and relentless. Nobody needs more of it. You need a straw."

Then I saw Karthi's post. And it stopped me cold. She Vibes by PiFo is not a course.

🌊

The Overwhelm That Started It All

Karthi had felt it too. She didn't pretend otherwise. "The AI era has been coming at me fast. I felt the overwhelm myself. And I realised how hard it is to just get started when the pace never slows down." That empathy — that honesty — is where Viber began. Not just AI. Not just learning. Not just community. All of it, together. A big AND equation where each part does the work the others cannot.

"Learning how to learn AND learning AI AND a community AND joy in the process."
🧪

Not a Course. A Behavior Change Program.

The post explained something I hadn't seen anyone else say clearly: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft have all built AI courses. And they're amazing. She Vibes is something different. It is a 66-day structure. Every week has a spine. Every day inside that week wraps a little more muscle around it. Same direction. Deeper each time. One byte a day. This is how the brain actually learns — not in big crashes of information, but in slow, specific, compounding layers.

Why 66 days? Because the research says it takes closer to 66 days for a new behavior to become automatic. Not forced. Not effortful. Just natural.

"We're not here to give you a skill. We're here to give you a habit that outlives the cohort. The 66 days end. The thinking doesn't."
🏗️

Built on Behavioral Science, Every Inch of It

This was the first time Karthi poured every behavioral science principle she knows into a single program. A lot of big bets, stacked together. She wasn't sure the AND would hold for 66 days.

But the design was deliberate: Attention span maxes out around 45 minutes a day — so they cut the lectures. Brains want to learn, not study — so they built experiences. People want a good deal — so they started free. Habits need time — so 66 days, with each day building on the last through progressive overload. And daily reflection, because learners have to connect their own dots.

"You don't feel overwhelmed because nothing is vague. You know exactly what you're doing today. And it connects to everything before it. Day 7 only makes sense if Days 1 through 6 have done their job."
📅

Mentor Wisdom

Words That Change Everything

Distilled from hundreds of office hours, morning sessions, and late-night conversations — the insights that shaped 240+ builders.

👩‍🏫 Karthi Subbaraman

AI as Amplifier

AI doesn't replace you — it amplifies you. Every tool, every shortcut, every automation makes the best version of you larger and louder.

"I am at least a hundred X of Karthi of what I was before — thanks to AI."

The Trust Index

Build your Trust Index with AI over time — just like any relationship. Start at 40–60%, observe outputs, validate, and let trust grow with evidence.

"Play enough with the tools so that you can understand the Trust Index."

Think · Do · Articulate

The three metaskills of the modern era. AI helps with Doing and Articulating — but Thinking is still yours to own. Never outsource your reasoning.

"It is a reasoning engine. It understands the context, but thinking is still our job."

We Are All Value Operators

Forget job titles. PMs, designers, engineers — we all create, deliver, communicate, and capture value. Whoever does this best, wins.

"There are no PMs, designers, and engineers. We are all value operators."

Trust But Verify

AI changed the way Karthi looks at everything. The default is no longer face value — it's trust but verify. Verify what you can; hold back on what you can't.

"I'm able to verify it and if I am not, I am not taking it on face value."

The Knife Analogy

Refusing AI because of dependence fears is like refusing a kitchen knife. AI is a tool — use it. The skill is knowing you remain you, and AI stays the tool.

"Are we saying we will not work with a knife in the kitchen?"

Privacy Is a Personal Choice

On the internet, all of us are naked. The honest cost-benefit: if you give no context, tools can't work. If you give everything, there are risks. Your call, always.

"There is always a way it can leak — be okay with that, or do not use it. The choice is ours."

Fresh Context Is Everything

A chat is a fresh context. Don't mix travel, health, and work in one thread — you confuse the AI. Use focused chats, projects with memory, and specific threads for specific goals.

"Keep the context with that one thing — don't make it a kichadi."

Known → Unknown Bridge

Learning only works when you bridge from the known to the unknown. Two unknowns with no bridge = overwhelm. Always anchor new learning to something already familiar.

"Known to unknown is the bridge. If it is unknown to unknown, there is no bridge."

Guard Against Cognitive Atrophy

Too much reliance isn't good for you either. Critical thinking is a human skill we must protect. Use AI to amplify, not to abdicate your reasoning entirely.

"Cognitive atrophy is real. Outsource the doing, never the thinking."

66 Days to Build the Muscle

The science of habits: 40% drop off in week one. Survive week one and you'll make it. 66 days is the number that turns a forced effort into an earned muscle.

"The first week is the hardest. If you survive it, you will go on well for the second."

Speed vs Accuracy Balance

Speed destroys accuracy; accuracy can kill speed. The art is finding the balance between the two — knowing when to move fast and when to slow down for quality.

"When things move fast, at least capture release notes of all your thinking and decisions."

The AI-Forward Professional

AI-forward isn't a title — it's a way of working. Show first, build second. Contract the cycle. Make iteration so cheap that the thinking becomes the bottleneck, not the doing.

"The barrier to doing is on the floor. Whoever orchestrates the moving parts wins."

Fire Parts of Yourself Daily

Every day, identify one thing AI can do instead of you. Delegate it. What remains is the irreplaceable you — the judgment, the taste, the creative spark that no model can replicate.

"See if you can fire a part of yourself every single day so that you don't have to do that job."

Learn How Learning Works

The brain is a processing engine, not a storage engine. Even if there's an ocean of content, you can only gulp it through a straw. Design your learning for the straw, not the ocean.

"Focus on getting the job done, not memorizing concepts."

Completion = Serotonin

Don't explore without finishing. Your evidence of completion builds confidence. Print the document. Complete the flow. Small wins compound into unshakeable momentum.

"Don't be stuck on trying new things and not finishing your goal. Give yourself serotonin with completion."
🧠 Mitalee — AI PM & Evals Lead

The New PM Superpower

AI PMs don't write long specs. They prototype super fast, find edge cases immediately, and write eval specs instead of PRDs. Show, don't tell.

"We don't write specs, we show. And we write 'eval specs' — what a user asks and what the answer should be."

Evals First, MVP Second

Start your AI product with an eval spec — not wireframes, not a PRD. Evals are your training set. They prevent scenario drift and keep your MVP grounded in real intent.

"Start with an eval spec — life will be vastly better than if you build an MVP and the original intent pivots."

Focus on Scenarios, Not LLMs

The craft of a PM in AI is not understanding which model is best — it's understanding scenarios and maximizing AI potential while minimizing scenario drift.

"AI PMs today have to focus on scenarios, rather than LLMs. I don't even look at hallucinations anymore."

Verification = Manual Evals

All output verification is called manual evals. Check the output. Compare against sourced answers from you or another expert. For math, always use a calculator or code. Never trust the LLM on numbers.

"Insight: you need to have sourced the right answers first — then verify with what the model gives you."

Everyone Does Evals Now

At Microsoft, designers do evals. Researchers do evals. When prototyping is possible by everyone, evaluation becomes the new differentiation — not the ability to build.

"The window to iteration with live customer feedback has been squeezed to the bare minimum — sometimes just 2 weeks."

Protect Your Critical Thinking

There was a period of just outsourcing everything. It cost Mitalee her critical thinking. New skills now: reviewing LLM outputs carefully, understanding what the AI writes, and sometimes going back to writing alone.

"I actually lost my critical thinking skills. It was a period of me just outsourcing stuff."
⚡ Adi — Platform & Engineering Lead

Zone of Genius vs Beyond

Anything beyond your zone of genius? Use AI without hesitation — it gets you to your goal faster. Inside your zone? AI handles the repeated parts, and you handle what only you can do.

"Using AI is a superpower any time there is a circle beyond your reach. We're living in a very good time right now."

Break the Workflow First

Before building any automation or skill: manually map your workflow block by block. Identify what can be automated (maybe 40%). Build a specific skill for that — not for the entire thing at once.

"Understand your current manual workflow, see what gets automated, then create a skill for that."

Start Absurdly Small

The best way to get going is to start super small. Get it working on one tiny thing. It's addictive. One win leads to the next, and before you know it, you've built something real.

"Start small. Like super small. It's so much fun, it's addictive."

Claude Cowork as a Starting Point

For automation beginners: use Claude Cowork. Ask it to fetch emails, read messages, browse pages. Play with it. You'll generate dozens of real use cases just from exploring.

"Just play around with that — you will get lots and lots of ideas. It's the best way to get started right now."
🌱 Sindhu Kandipati — Learning & Strategy

Create Your Own Learning Plan

Explore broadly, then narrow down. Try design, product, engineering tools. Pick the areas most relevant to you and go deep there. You can't know everything — decide to be a generalist or specialist.

"It's impossible to know everything. Deciding whether you want to be a generalist or specialist can help you stay focused."

Learn by Building, Not Reading

Build a few prototypes. Experiment. The hands-on experience compounds faster than any course. Use AI to accelerate that learning — it removes the barrier of technical skill from the loop.

"I explored a range of areas before narrowing my focus. Eventually I decided to build prototypes and learn by doing."

Ask Probing Questions of AI Output

For important queries: ask what assumptions are being made, what could be wrong, and what the margin of error is. View the answer from a product lens, a technical lens, a business lens.

"For anything important: cross-check, question, and do a quick real-world sanity check. Adjust the level of validation to the impact."

Roles Are Blurring — Expand Your Reach

QA, PM, Design, Engineering — the lines are actively dissolving. Differentiation now comes from domain knowledge, judgment, and the ability to connect AI to real business problems.

"Use AI to your advantage — it can significantly accelerate your learning. What truly differentiates you is still you."
Frameworks

Models for Thinking & Building

Reusable mental models that surfaced across 66 days of shared learning — apply them to your work today.

Think · Do · Articulate

The three metaskills of the AI era. AI accelerates Do and Articulate — but Thinking is irreplaceable. Invest more time in thinking well, because AI execution is getting cheaper every day.

The Trust Index

Build your personal trust calibration for each AI tool. Start conservative (40–60%), watch outputs, validate against ground truth, and increment trust as evidence accumulates. Never set it to 100%.

Eval-First Development

Before writing code or wireframes, write an eval spec: what the user asks + what the ideal answer looks like. This becomes your north star and prevents scope creep, hallucination drift, and rework.

Value Operator Model

Strip away titles. Every professional creates, delivers, communicates, and captures value. AI changes the tools, not the mission. Whoever operationalizes value creation fastest will lead in the AI era.

Known → Unknown Bridge

Learning happens only when you have one known and one unknown. Connect them. Two unknowns with no bridge leads to overwhelm and dropout. Always anchor new concepts to something already understood.

Workflow Breakdown Before Automation

Map every step of your manual workflow first. Block by block. Identify which 40% could be automated. Then build a targeted skill for that 40% — don't try to automate everything at once.

Zone of Genius Framework

Everything within your zone of genius: protect it, maybe automate repetitive parts. Everything outside it: use AI freely and aggressively. The goal is to operate exclusively within your genius zone.

Privacy Cost-Benefit Model

Every data-sharing decision is a trade. Weigh the benefit of better AI output against the cost of exposure. Make the decision consciously, not by default. Your data strategy is a personal constitution.

The 8 Levels of Prompting

Zero-shot → One-shot → Few-shot → Role prompting → Chain of thought → Prompt chaining → System prompts → Meta prompting. Each level unlocks a new dimension of AI capability and output quality.

Show Don't Tell Prompting

Two well-chosen examples beat three paragraphs of instruction every time. When prompting AI, show the format, tone, and structure you want — don't describe it. Examples are the most efficient context.

Context Engineering Model

AI is only as good as its context. Give it your documents, calendar, emails, and domain knowledge. The shift from casual conversation to integrated workflow happens when context becomes continuous.

Speed vs Accuracy Balance

Fast prototypes generate learning; slow verification ensures quality. For exploration, optimize speed. For production, optimize accuracy. The mistake is applying the wrong mode to the wrong phase.

AI as Mirror Principle

The quality of AI output reflects the clarity of your thinking. A bad response isn't a failure — it's information about a gap in your prompt or reasoning. You are always the variable that matters most.

7 E's Learning Framework

Pay for the first four stages of learning (Exposure, Exploration, Experience, Evaluation). Get paid for the last three (Expertise, Expression, Elevation). The goal of Viber is to take you from the first four to the last three.

Key Insights

Critical Truths from 66 Days

Condensed lessons that took months of building, failing, questioning, and reflecting to arrive at.

🪞

AI Is a Mirror of Your Clarity

Bad output from AI means unclear thinking from you. Fix the thinking; the output will follow. The model is rarely the problem — the prompt almost always is.

🧠

You Are Still Required

AI takes over doing and articulating — judgment, taste, and decision-making remain exclusively human. The professional who combines both will be unstoppable.

🌐

Context Is Everything

A model without context is a powerful tool in an empty room. Feed it your world — your documents, goals, constraints — and watch the output transform from generic to irreplaceable.

🔨

Learn by Building

Reading about AI is 10% as effective as building with it. Ship something broken. Iterate. Ship something better. The loop is the learning — there is no other way.

⚖️

Privacy Is Always a Trade

There is no free lunch with data. Every AI interaction involves a cost-benefit decision. Make it consciously — don't share names or sensitive specifics you wouldn't want exposed.

🚀

Collective Level-Up Is Real

When one person on a team levels up with AI, everyone benefits. When the whole team levels up together, the velocity compound becomes almost impossible for others to match.

✅

Completion Builds Confidence

Exploring ten half-built things leaves you with ten fragments and no confidence. Finishing one small thing — really finishing it — builds more capacity than any course.

🎯

Evals Are the New Specs

For AI products, eval specs replace PRDs. They describe success criteria in terms of user input and ideal output — grounding the team in real behavior, not theoretical features.

⚡

Speed ≠ Accuracy

AI makes doing fast, but fast execution of a wrong direction is just faster failure. The thinking phase must get slower and more deliberate, even as the execution phase accelerates.

🧩

Model Blending Works

Different models have different strengths. Blend them. Use one for generation, another for critique, another for research. The orchestrator who knows when to use which tool wins.

🌱

Learning Happens Gradually, Then Suddenly

Week one feels impossible. Week three feels natural. Week six feels obvious. The curve is not linear. Trust the discomfort — it means you are on the right side of the learning graph.

🔁

Amplify, Don't Abdicate

The most dangerous AI habit is using it to replace thinking entirely. The highest-value use is using it to amplify thinking — so your best ideas spread farther and faster than ever before.

Best Practices

How to Build & Work with AI

Practical patterns that emerged from 66 days of real builds, real failures, and real breakthroughs.

When Building with AI

1

Start with a Real Use Case

Don't search for problems that fit your solution. Start with a genuine problem you live with, and let that drive the build. Real use cases produce real learning.

2

Write Eval Specs First

Before touching a line of code or a design tool, define: what does the user ask, and what should the ideal response look like? This is your north star and your quality gate.

3

Give Complete Context

AI performs at the level of context you provide. Give it your role, your constraints, your data, your audience. A context-rich prompt beats a clever prompt every time.

4

Map the Manual Workflow First

Before automating anything, walk through the workflow manually. Step by step. Only then can you see what's worth automating, and what still needs the human touch.

5

Validate Every Output

Especially for math, facts, and high-stakes decisions. Use a calculator. Cross-reference. Ask follow-up questions. Trust but verify is not optional — it's the practice.

6

Document Your Decisions

Even if you're moving fast, capture release notes of your thinking. When prototypes are non-deterministic, a decision trail is the only way to understand how you got here.

7

Prioritize Quick Wins

Build parts of your solution and see them working. That working piece is your quick win. Big complex visions are overwhelming — quick wins keep momentum and confidence alive.

8

Build Gradually, Not All at Once

Focus on works first, looks last. Functional beats beautiful at the start. Ship something real, get feedback, iterate. The best builders start embarrassingly small.

When Working in Teams

1

Everyone Levels Up Together

An AI-forward PM deserves an AI-forward designer. An AI-forward designer deserves an AI-forward engineer. If one wheel is slow, the whole car slows down. Level up as a unit.

2

Run Cross-Functional Evals

Evaluation is no longer a QA job. PMs, designers, researchers, and engineers all participate. Build shared evals that everyone can contribute to and everyone can trust.

3

Think as Value Operators

Strip away role silos. Everyone asks: how does this create, deliver, and communicate value? This question — not job description — should drive every decision on an AI-forward team.

4

Hold Regular Sync Points

Async AI work creates divergence fast. Regular sync moments — even 30 minutes weekly — keep the team aligned on what's working, what's drifting, and what needs a course correction.

5

Share What You're Learning

Every discovery, every prompt trick, every shortcut — share it in a shared space. The team that shares its learning compounds faster than the team of individual learners working alone.

6

Set Boundaries for Your Genius

Define what only you can do on the team. Protect that. Automate everything else. When everyone operates from their zone of genius, the team becomes exponentially more powerful.
